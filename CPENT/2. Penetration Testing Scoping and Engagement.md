# Penetration Testing: Pre-engagement Activities

**Pre-engagement Activities** are the **planning and preparation steps** performed **before** starting a penetration test. They ensure the test is **authorized, well-planned, and successful**.

- Establishes the foundation for a successful penetration test.
- Defines the goals, scope, and rules of the engagement.
- Ensures both the client and tester understand their responsibilities.
- Prevents scope creep (testing beyond the agreed scope).
- Reduces misunderstandings and customer dissatisfaction.
- Helps avoid legal and compliance issues.
- Ensures proper planning before testing begins.
- Starts by defining the objective (goal) of the penetration test.

## Request for Proposal (RFP)

**RFP (Request for Proposal)** is a **formal document** that an organization sends to penetration testing companies to **request proposals** for performing a penetration test.

## What is Included in an RFP?

##### 1. Purpose of the RFP

Defines **why** the penetration test is required.

**Includes:**

- Evaluate the organization's security.
- Identify security vulnerabilities.
- Assess business risks.
- Receive recommendations for improving security.

##### 2. Technical & Contractual Contact

Provides the contact details for communication.

**Includes:**

- Technical contact person
- Contract manager
- Email address
- Phone number
- Scope of Work (SOW)
- Contract terms and conditions

##### 3. Schedule of Events

Defines the timeline for the engagement.

**Includes:**

- RFP release date
- Deadline for vendor questions
- Proposal submission deadline
- Proposal review date
- Vendor selection date
- Project start date

## Proposal Submission

A **Proposal Submission** is a document prepared by a **penetration testing vendor** in response to an **RFP (Request for Proposal)**. It explains **how the vendor will perform the penetration test** and why they should be selected.

### Elements of a Proposal Submission

##### 1. Executive Summary

- Provides a **high-level overview** of the proposed penetration testing engagement.
- Explains the objectives, features, and benefits of the testing.

##### 2. Project Deliverables

- Lists the **documents and reports** the client will receive.
- Examples:
    - Executive Summary Report
    - Technical Report
    - Remediation Report

##### 3. Itemized Pricing

- Provides a **detailed cost breakdown** of the penetration testing services.
- Explains how the total cost is calculated.

##### 4. Team Strength

- Introduces the **testing team**.
- Includes:
    - Team members
    - Certifications
    - Skills
    - Experience

##### 5. Approach and Methodology

- Explains **how the penetration test will be performed**.
- Includes:
    - Testing methodology
    - Tools and techniques
    - Testing phases
    - Technical approach

##### 6. Project Management

- Describes **how the project will be planned, managed, and completed**.
- Includes:
    - Timeline
    - Communication process
    - Milestones
    - Responsibilities

##### 7. References

- Lists **previous clients or similar projects** completed by the company.
- Demonstrates the vendor's experience and credibility.

##### 8. Company Briefing

- Provides basic information about the penetration testing company.
- Includes:
    - Company profile
    - Address
    - Contact details
    - Services offered
    - Areas of expertise

## Pen Test Scoping Meeting

A **Pen Test Scoping Meeting** is a meeting between the **client and the penetration testing team** to define and confirm the **scope, objectives, and requirements** of the penetration test before testing begins.

`Rules of Engagement (ROE) and cost are not discuss in this meeting`

- A scoping meeting is conducted after the penetration testing goal is identified.
- The purpose of the meeting is to define the scope of the penetration test.
- The meeting may be held after the contract is signed.
- Sometimes the meeting is held before the contract, and the client may require an NDA to be signed first.
- The RFP (Request for Proposal) is reviewed to understand the client's requirements.
- The client's expectations, scope, type, and category of the penetration test are confirmed.
- A questionnaire is used to gather additional information and clarify requirements.
- Questions are asked to accurately estimate the testing scope.

`NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement) - A legal agreement that protects the client's confidential information from being disclosed.`

## Listing Tests That Will Not Be Performed

- Identify and document the tests that are not allowed before the penetration test begins.
- This prevents misunderstandings between the client and the penetration testing team.

#### Social Engineering Penetration Testing

1. **Define the pretexts (scenarios) that are not allowed during social engineering testing.**
2. **Pretexts involving sex, drugs, pornography, etc., are generally not allowed in corporate environments.**
3. **Obtain written approval from the client for all approved pretexts before testing begins.**

**Pretext:** A fake story or identity used by a penetration tester to trick a target into revealing information or performing an action.

#### Denial of Service (DoS) Testing

1. **DoS testing may make the client's services unavailable.**
2. **Organizations (especially e-commerce companies) may choose not to allow DoS testing.**
3. **DoS testing should only be performed if the client explicitly requests it.**
4. **Written approval must be obtained before performing DoS testing.**

## Estimating the Cost of the Penetration Test Engagement

|**Topic**|**Explanation (Simple Terms)**|
|---|---|
|**Cost Estimation**|The cost of a penetration test depends on the **scope, resources, and time** required.|
|**Scope**|Larger or more complex projects require more testing, increasing the cost.|
|**Resources**|More penetration testers, tools, and infrastructure increase the cost.|
|**Time Required**|Longer testing duration (hours/days) results in higher cost.|
|**Pricing Method**|Charges are usually negotiated **per hour** or **per day**.|
|**Project Pricing**|The total cost depends on the **scope of work** and the **number of man-days/hours** required.|
|**Salary/Payment**|Payment is negotiated by the lead penetration tester or project manager according to the client agreement.|
|**Final Cost**|**Higher scope + More resources + More time = Higher cost.**|

## Points of Contact (POC) Template

**POC (Point of Contact)** is the **person or department responsible for communication and coordination** during the penetration testing engagement.

|**Topic**|**Explanation (Simple Terms)**|
|---|---|
|**POC (Point of Contact)**|A person or department responsible for communication during the penetration testing project.|
|**Purpose**|Acts as the main contact between the client and the penetration testing team.|
|**Who can be a POC?**|An individual (e.g., Project Manager) or a department (e.g., IT or Security Team).|
|**Responsibility**|Coordinates activities, shares information, and resolves issues during the engagement.|
|**Importance**|Ensures communication is quick, accurate, and timely.|
|**When Used**|Used when information is **time-sensitive** and immediate communication is required.|

## ROE (Rules of Engagement)

**ROE (Rules of Engagement)** is a **formal document** that defines **how a penetration test will be conducted**, including the **scope, permissions, limitations, and responsibilities** of both the client and the penetration testing team.

- Prevents unauthorized testing.
- Defines the boundaries of the penetration test.
- Protects both the client and the penetration tester legally.
- Reduces the risk of service disruption.
- Ensures testing is performed safely and professionally.
