# Wireless Standard

A **wireless standard** is a set of rules and specifications defined by **IEEE** that tells wireless devices **how to communicate**, **what frequency to use**, **what speed to support**, and **whether devices can work together**.

`What is IEEE ?`

`IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers) is an international professional organization that develops and publishes standards for technology, networking, electronics, telecommunications, and computing.`

`Many of the networking standards used today are created by IEEE.`

`IEEE creates standards that ensure devices from different manufacturers can communicate and work together properly.` 

Imagine different mobile chargers:

- USB-C charger follows one standard.
- Lightning charger follows another standard.

If two devices follow the same standard, they can work together.

Similarly, wireless devices follow standards like **802.11a, 802.11b, 802.11g, and 802.11n**.

---
## IEEE 802.11 Standards

**IEEE 802.11** is a family of standards developed by **IEEE** for **Wireless Local Area Networks (WLANs)**, commonly known as **Wi-Fi**.

These standards define how wireless devices communicate over radio waves.

### 802.11 (Original WiFi)

**802.11** was the first WiFi standard created by IEEE in 1997.

- Frequency: **2.4 GHz**
- Maximum Speed: **2 Mbps**
- First wireless networking standard.
- Foundation for all future WiFi standards.

### 802.11a

**802.11a** is a wireless standard that operates on the **5 GHz** frequency band.

- Frequency: **5 GHz**
- Maximum Speed: **54 Mbps**
- Less interference than 2.4 GHz.
- More available channels.
- Faster than 802.11b.

### 802.11b

**802.11b** is a wireless standard that operates on the **2.4 GHz** frequency band.

- Frequency: **2.4 GHz**
- Maximum Speed: **11 Mbps**
- Longer range than 802.11a.
- Compatible with 802.11g and 802.11n.

### 802.11g

**802.11g** is an improved version of 802.11b that provides higher speed while keeping compatibility.

- Frequency: **2.4 GHz**
- Maximum Speed: **54 Mbps**
- Backward compatible with 802.11b.
- Uses the same frequency as 802.11b.
- Faster than 802.11b.
- Compatible with older 802.11b devices.

### 802.11n (Wi-Fi 4)

**802.11n** is a high-speed wireless standard that introduced **MIMO** and **Channel Bonding** technologies.

`MIMO (Multiple Input Multiple Output)`

- `MIMO (Multiple Input Multiple Output) is a wireless technology that uses multiple antennas at both the transmitter and receiver to send and receive multiple data streams simultaneously.`
- `MIMO increases the speed, range, and reliability of wireless communication.`

`Channel Bonding`

- `Channel Bonding is a technology that combines two or more wireless channels into a single wider channel to increase data transfer speed.`

- Frequency: **2.4 GHz and 5 GHz**
- Maximum Speed: **600 Mbps**
- Compatible with 802.11a, b, and g.
- Much faster, better signal quality & better coverage.

### 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5)

**802.11ac** is a high-performance wireless standard designed for modern networks.

- Frequency: **5 GHz**
- Maximum Speed: **Up to 6.9 Gbps**
- Improved MIMO technology.
- Wider wireless channels.

### 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6)

**802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6)** is the latest mainstream WiFi standard designed for high-density environments.

- Frequency: **2.4 GHz and 5 GHz**
- Maximum Speed: **Up to 9.6 Gbps**
- Better performance with many devices.
- Lower latency.
- Better battery efficiency.

Allows multiple devices to communicate simultaneously on the same channel.
