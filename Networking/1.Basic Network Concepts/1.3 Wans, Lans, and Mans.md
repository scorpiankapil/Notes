A Local Area Network (LAN) is a network that covers a small area such as a home, office, school, building, or college campus. LANs are fast and commonly used for connecting nearby devices and sharing resources within the same location.

A Wide Area Network (WAN) covers a very large geographical area such as cities, countries, or the entire world. A WAN is usually created by connecting multiple LANs together. For example, if a company has one office in Halifax and another office in Toronto, each office has its own LAN. When both LANs are connected to share information, the network becomes a WAN. The Internet is the biggest example of a WAN.

A Metropolitan Area Network (MAN) covers a city or large campus area. It is larger than a LAN but smaller than a WAN. If two buildings within the same city are connected together, it is considered a MAN.

- LAN = small area network
- WAN = connects multiple LANs over large distances
- MAN = network within a city
- Internet is the largest WAN
